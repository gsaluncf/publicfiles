Case 05 / Movie recommendations

Competing on Analytics

Davenport and Harris · Netflix movie recommendations

Netflix subscribers chose DVDs from a catalog. The company recommended movies by predicting which titles each subscriber would enjoy. In 2006, Netflix offered a prize for a more accurate way to predict movie ratings.

Netflix Prize
Prize $1 million
Required reduction in error 10%
Netflix awarded the prize in 2009.

Movie recommendations

Thomas Davenport and Jeanne Harris describe how companies select an activity central to their business and invest in the analysis that supports it. Netflix chose movie recommendations. 02

Ratings withheld for testing

Netflix released more than 100 million movie ratings to participants in the Netflix Prize. It withheld some ratings so it could evaluate predictions. Each team submitted a predicted rating for each subscriber and movie in the test. Netflix compared those predictions with the stars the subscribers had given. Netflix’s account of the competition.

Netflix measured prediction error in stars. For each prediction, it squared the difference between the predicted rating and the subscriber’s rating. It averaged the squared differences and took the square root. A lower root mean squared error indicated more accurate predictions.

03

The winning score

Cinematch scored 0.9525 on the final test. BellKor’s Pragmatic Chaos scored 0.8567 and won the $1 million prize. The competitors saw scores for a separate set of ratings during the contest. Netflix used the final test scores to award the prize. A competitor’s account of the final scores.

04

The engineering decision

Netflix engineers put two methods from an earlier stage of the contest into the recommendation service. They also tested techniques from the final winning entry. The engineers judged the additional improvement too small to justify the work required to put those techniques into service. Netflix engineers’ explanation.

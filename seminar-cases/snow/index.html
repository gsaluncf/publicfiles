01

London’s water suppliers

The Lambeth Water Company moved its intake upstream to a cleaner part of the Thames in 1852. The Southwark and Vauxhall Water Company continued to draw water from a part of the river polluted by sewage. One house could receive water from Lambeth and the house next door from Southwark and Vauxhall. UCLA’s Snow archive.

02

Identifying the water supplier

Snow visited the addresses in the death records. He asked residents which company supplied their water. He checked their answers against receipts for water payments. He also tested water samples. Water from Southwark and Vauxhall contained more salt than water from Lambeth.

Some houses had no number. Others shared a number or had been renumbered. When a patient fell ill at home and died in an institution, Snow needed the address where the illness began. Snow’s 1856 account.

03

Cholera deaths per 10,000 houses

Snow counted cholera deaths among each company’s customers during the first seven weeks of the outbreak. Southwark and Vauxhall supplied more houses than Lambeth. Snow calculated deaths per 10,000 houses so he could compare the two groups. Table IX.

Southwark and Vauxhall had 315.4 cholera deaths per 10,000 houses. Lambeth had 37.5 per 10,000 houses. The death rate among Southwark and Vauxhall households was 8.4 times the rate among Lambeth households.

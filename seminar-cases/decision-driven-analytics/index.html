Case 09 / Customer retention

Decisions, not data, should drive analytics programs

MIT Sloan · Wireless plan recommendations

A South American wireless provider could identify customers who regularly spent more than their monthly plans covered. Its managers wanted to know whether recommending a better-fitting plan would persuade those customers to stay.

01

The retention decision

Beth Stackpole’s MIT Sloan article describes how to choose data for a decision. Managers first identify the actions available to them. Then they decide which outcome would favor one action over another.

Researchers Eva Ascarza, Raghuram Iyengar, and Martin Schleicher studied that decision at a wireless provider. Staff could call customers to recommend a different plan or continue their usual service. The provider wanted to retain customers. The researchers therefore needed to measure how many customers left after the campaign.

02

Monthly bills and plan changes

The researchers used three months of billing records to identify customers who could have spent less under one of the recommended plans. They randomly assigned eligible customers to a recommendation group or a control group. Customers in the control group continued with their usual service.

A customer could switch to another plan with the same provider. Table 2 reports those switches for both groups. Each percentage uses the total number of customers assigned to that group.

03

Customers who left

The researchers separately recorded customers who left the provider within three months. They called this outcome churn. Customers assigned to receive a recommendation changed plans more often. They also left the provider more often.

The comparison includes everyone assigned to receive a recommendation, including customers who declined the offer. Customers who accepted made that choice themselves. Comparing only those customers with the control group would lose the benefit of random assignment.

04

The campaign recommendation

A campaign manager could see an increase in plan changes and recommend more calls. A manager responsible for retention needs the cancellation rates for the same groups and the same period.

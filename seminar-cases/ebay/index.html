Case 03 / Marketing analytics

eBay and paid search advertising

eBay paid Google when a shopper clicked one of its search ads. Researchers wanted to know how much the ads increased total sales.

01

Sales after an ad click

The researchers counted a purchase as an attributed sale if the shopper bought something within 24 hours of clicking a Google search ad. The researchers called search terms without the name “eBay” non-brand keywords. They stopped the ads in 68 markets and kept them running in 142 comparison markets. Blake, Nosko, and Tadelis, pp. 8 to 10.

02

Searches for eBay

The researchers ran a separate test for shoppers who included “eBay” in their search. When the paid ads stopped, shoppers reached eBay through unpaid search results instead. The increase in visits from unpaid search results accounted for nearly all the visits lost when the ads stopped. A shopper who already planned to buy could click an ad and make a purchase that counted as an attributed sale. Berkeley Haas account.

03

Attributed sales and total sales

Figure 3 shows the results for non-brand keywords. In Panel A, sales attributed to ad clicks fell by more than 72% in markets where the ads stopped. Panel B shows how total sales differed between markets with and without the ads. The researchers estimated that the ads increased total sales by 0.66%. The 95% confidence interval ranged from −0.42% to 1.74%. Figure 3 and results.

04

Return on investment

The researchers compared the additional revenue from the ads with the cost of running them. They estimated a return on investment (ROI) of 1,632% using observational data with statistical controls. They estimated a return of −63% using the market experiment. Table 1.

The ads attracted new customers and increased purchases among people who shopped at eBay infrequently. The researchers found no statistically significant change in purchases among frequent customers. ISMS case summary.

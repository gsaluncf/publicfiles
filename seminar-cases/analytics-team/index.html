Case 07 / Analytics staffing

How to build a data analytics dream team

MIT Sloan · Pfizer shipping schedules

01

The shipping decision

Shipping planners used spreadsheets to assign batches of medicine to trucks. Each assignment needed a destination and shipping date. Some destinations accepted deliveries only on certain days. Some products required a truck equipped for dry ice.

Emma Beal and Amelia Whitworth worked with Pfizer’s digital manufacturing team to build a scheduling tool. Their project poster names both students, a faculty advisor, and three company advisors. Project poster.

02

Preparing the records

A batch record tells a planner what the plant has produced. An order tells the planner what a customer needs. A batch could appear in the production records while staff were still checking its quality.

The team filtered the batch records for medicine ready to ship and the order records for demand due that week. Brown describes preparing data for use by others as part of a data engineer’s work. A generalist analyst can prepare the records and analyze them. Sara Brown on analytics roles.

03

Testing the schedule

Beal and Whitworth used three weeks of historical orders to compare their proposed schedules with the original plans. They measured how much demand each schedule met, how much shipping cost, and how evenly the work was distributed.

A shipping planner also needed to see which truck would carry a particular order. The team designed a display that grouped batches by order and vehicle. Brown says a data translator explains analytical results in terms that business staff can use. MIT Sloan’s project account.

04

Explaining the difference

Meeting more demand can change the workload on the shipping floor. In the second week, the proposed schedule met more demand. It also increased the variation in workload. A planner needs both figures to decide whether to use that schedule.
